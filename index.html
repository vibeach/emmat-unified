01 · The Research

Three emulsifiers. Seven experimental series. Three finished products.

The research question

Three natural oil-in-water emulsifiers — an ester blend, an alkyl polyglucoside and a hydrophobically modified starch — put through the same seven experimental series, characterised on the same bench, profiled by the same five noses and hands. The point of the exercise was to see, without shortcuts, what each system can actually do, and to let the answer decide which finished product it belongs in.

Natural emulsifiers are quietly replacing the synthetic surfactants that defined the last century of cosmetic chemistry. Clean-beauty demand and sustainability standards set the direction; skinification and K-beauty raise the bar on lightness, absorption and multifunctionality. What has not moved is the underlying difficulty — natural emulsions that hold across different oils, pigments and actives remain a genuine formulation challenge.

02 · The Three Emulsifiers

Three natural systems, three distinct technical identities

i

Ester Blend

Sorbitan Laurate / Polyglyceryl-4 Laurate / Dilauryl Citrate

Non-ionic ester system

A three-component non-ionic ester blend that works through classical interfacial-film mechanics. It asks for Xanthan Gum and Cetearyl Alcohol together, at 4%, and rewards the effort with the lightest, most fluid and most spreadable texture of the three — and the lowest soaping effect on skin.

→ Assigned to BB Cream Anti-Pollution

ii

Alkyl Polyglucoside

C14-22 Alcohols / C12-20 Alkyl Glucoside

APG + fatty alcohol · self-structuring lamellar network

An APG–fatty alcohol pair that self-assembles into lamellar liquid-crystal networks, carrying its own structure even before a thickener is added. It takes oil loads up to 30% in its stride and finishes rich, occlusive and quietly luminous — the highest shine and residual film of the three.

→ Assigned to Anti-Age Body Cream

iii

Modified Starch

Sodium Starch Octenylsuccinate

Pickering emulsion · surfactant-free

A hydrophobically modified starch that anchors the interface with solid particles rather than surfactant — a Pickering emulsion in the classical sense, stable at 5% with no added surfactant at all. Fastest to absorb, lightest to leave behind, with a distinctive powdery after-feel that reads as clean skin.

→ Assigned to Milky Toner

03 · Methodology

Seven controlled experimental series, evaluated in parallel

The same seven series, the same instrumental panel, the same five-person sensory jury — applied identically to each of the three systems so that any difference in the finished formulation can be traced back to the emulsifier itself, and to nothing else.

S1

Baseline Stability

Mixed oil phase ± Xanthan Gum and Cetearyl Alcohol stabilisation.

S2

Oil Phase Load

10 %, 20 %, 30 % oil fraction to map upper stability limits.

S3

Emollient Category

Alkane, ester, triglyceride, silicone-alternative — single-oil trials.

S4

Emulsifier Concentration

Supplier-recommended 2 % vs. doubled 4 % to size headroom.

S5

Rheology Modifiers

Xanthan Gum, Xanthan Gum Crosspolymer-2, Hydroxyethylcellulose, carbomer.

S6

Preparation Method

Turbo-emulsifier, cold-process (Lin), hot/cold interphase.

S7

Pigmented Emulsion

Trichromatic inorganic pigments — pigment compatibility screen.

Characterisation panel

pH (t0 / t24h) Viscosity @ 2.5 / 5 / 10 rpm Centrifuge stability Optical microscopy 5-panellist sensory profile
04 · Key Findings

Each emulsifier maps to one product family

Ester Blend
  • Only becomes stable once Xanthan Gum and Cetearyl Alcohol are combined.
  • Best performance at 4 % concentration; 2 % undergoes delayed but complete phase separation after two months.
  • Coco-Caprylate/Caprate is the most compatible oil at 10–20 % load.
  • Light, fluid, highly spreadable — lowest soaping effect of the three systems.
  • Excellent pigment compatibility: colour homogeneity 7.3 / 10, blendability 7.6 / 10.
Alkyl Polyglucoside
  • Builds lamellar liquid-crystal networks with intrinsic structuring, even without added stabilisers.
  • Tolerates oil loads up to 30 %; performs best at 4 % emulsifier concentration.
  • Rich, occlusive texture — highest shine (7.4) and residual film (7.1) of the three.
  • Incompatible with inorganic pigments (immediate separation).
  • Best long-term behaviour across the three-month stability study.
Modified Starch
  • Stabilises through a Pickering mechanism — stable at 5 % without added stabilisers.
  • Tolerates a broad range of oils; reaches very high viscosities even at low oil fractions.
  • Fastest-absorbing on skin (rate 6.5); lowest residual amount (3.6); distinctive powdery after-feel.
  • Pigmented version stayed stable under centrifugation but showed oil migration on storage.
  • Visually stable over three months but lost ~59 % of initial viscosity.

Interfacial-film mechanics · lamellar liquid-crystal co-assembly · Pickering-type particle adsorption. Three mechanisms, three product families.

05 · Sensory Analysis

A five-panellist quantitative descriptive profile

Protocol

Five of us, at the bench, blind. Samples coded, order randomised, every attribute scored on a continuous 0–10 scale in a controlled room. We worked through pickup and spread on the back of the hand, followed each formulation through the soaping phase, timed absorption, and finished on the residue — how much film remained, how it caught the light, how the skin felt an hour later. The pigmented series was read the same way, with coverage, blendability and mattifying effect added to the panel.

06 · The Products

Three finished cosmetics, one for each emulsifier

EMMAT Cosmetics × Cassiopea by Tiziana Terenzi — pastel iridescent glass, embossed with a modular motif drawn from hand-crocheted granny squares.

Make-up · 30 ml glass dropper

BB Cream Anti-Pollution

Light-to-medium coverage. A triple defence against the urban day.

A tinted O/W emulsion combining light-to-medium coverage with a scientifically coherent, multi-mechanism anti-pollution active system.

Emulsifier Ester Blend @ 4 %
pH (t0 / t24h) 6.7 / 6.8
Viscosity 8,960 → 19,520 mPa·s
Droplet size Uniform 2–5 µm

Anti-pollution active system

Ectoin — cyclo-amino-acid extremolyte from Halomonas elongata. Stabilises lipid-bilayer fluidity, protects heat-shock protein expression, reduces IL-1β and TNF-α in keratinocytes exposed to PM2.5 and diesel exhaust particles.
On skin: a quiet cushion of hydration that softens the reactive tightness of an urban day.
Lespedeza Capitata — flavonoid-rich temperate legume. Mitigates AhR-mediated disruption of the epidermal circadian clock, preserving nocturnal antioxidant capacity and diurnal barrier repair.
On skin: the sense of skin resetting to its own rhythm — fresher in the morning, calmer at night.
Camellia Japonica — polyphenolic catechins, gallic-acid derivatives, anthocyanin precursors. Direct scavenging of superoxide, hydroxyl and singlet oxygen; inhibition of MMP-1 in UVA-exposed fibroblasts.
On skin: a clean, unperfumed brightness — the polyphenolic signature of the flower without any of its floral weight.

Why this emulsifier for this product

Of the three systems, only the ester blend passed Series 7 with credible pigmented performance — a colour-homogeneity of 7.3 and a blendability of 7.6, against an APG platform that separated on contact with inorganic pigment and a starch platform that migrated on storage. Its sensory identity — light, fluid, the lowest soaping effect of the three — matched exactly what a tinted facial fluid needs to feel like at the point of application. Doubling from the supplier-recommended 2% to 4%, and locking in the Xanthan Gum plus Cetearyl Alcohol pair, closed the residual stability gap seen at 2% and produced the fine, uniform 2–5 µm droplet population documented under the microscope. Lowering the emulsification temperature from 75 °C to 60 °C protected the emulsifier itself — a preliminary trial finding, integrated into the finished process.

Body care · 200 ml glass pump

Anti-Age Body Cream

A rich, lamellar body cream that asks the skin to rebuild.

A richer, structuring O/W emulsion for daily anti-age body care — generous emollient load, clinically supported cosmeceutical actives package.

Emulsifier Alkyl Polyglucoside @ 4 %
pH (t0 / t24h) 6.3 / 6.3
Viscosity 98,000 → 91,600 mPa·s
Droplet size Coarse but uniform, 2–20 µm

Anti-age active system

Matrixyl → Matrikine KTTKTS (Palmitoyl Tripeptide-38) — palmitoylated collagen α2-derived signal peptide. Stimulates simultaneous synthesis of six ECM components in dermal fibroblasts: collagen I, III, IV, fibronectin, hyaluronic acid, laminin-5. Delivered in a hydroxypropyl cyclodextrin cavity complex.
On skin: the day-after firmness of a body that has been asked to rebuild, not just moisturised.
Niacinamide — NAD⁺/NADP⁺ precursor. Stimulates de novo ceramide biosynthesis via serine palmitoyl transferase, inhibits melanosome transfer through PGE2 and TXB2 suppression, reduces TEWL at 2–5 % topical concentration.
On skin: an even, quieted tone — the redness of an ordinary week reading down over time.
Allantoin — keratolytic and skin-conditioning agent. Loosens corneocyte cohesion for controlled desquamation, promotes fibroblast and keratinocyte proliferation, softens dry surface keratin.
On skin: dry surface keratin loosens; the texture of shins and elbows softens under the hand.

Why this emulsifier for this product

The APG system builds its own scaffolding — lamellar liquid-crystal networks that structure the finished cream without leaning on external thickeners. Series 4 confirmed it holds oil loads up to 30% (Trial 16), which is exactly the emollient budget an anti-age body format needs to carry shea butter, structured triglycerides and olive unsaponifiables. The very sensory signature that ruled it out for a facial product — 7.4 shine, 7.1 residual film — is the correct answer for body care, where the skin wants richness on the surface. Emulsification at 75 °C was non-negotiable: it melts the butter and the fatty-alcohol wax cleanly, and the resulting cream reaches 98,000 mPa·s at t0 and holds inside 7% of that after 24 hours.

Hybrid toner · 150 ml glass bottle · orifice-reducer cap

Milky Toner

The first layer, poured slow. Hydration, ceramides, calm.

A lightweight, lotion-like leave-on positioned between a conventional aqueous toner and a fluid emulsion — hydration and stratum-corneum barrier support immediately after cleansing.

Emulsifier Modified Starch @ 2.5 %
pH (t0 / t24h) 6.2 / 6.2
Viscosity 15,160 → 18,920 mPa·s
Droplet size Fine, 2–10 µm

Barrier-focused active system

Sodium Hyaluronate (0.1–0.5 %, 800 kDa – 1.2 MDa) — viscoelastic surface hydration reservoir, reinforces the natural moisturising factor pool, works synergistically with glycerin and butylene glycol without tackiness.
On skin: a still, plumped surface — hydration held without any of the tack that a single-humectant formula tends to leave.
Centella Asiatica — standardised to madecassoside and asiaticoside. Suppresses NF-κB nuclear translocation and pro-inflammatory cytokine cascades; asiaticoside stimulates type I collagen synthesis in dermal fibroblasts.
On skin: the small heat of post-cleansing reactivity settles almost immediately.
Lactobacillus Ferment (maltodextrin fermented) — postbiotic short-chain lactic acid oligomers, low-molecular-weight peptides, cell-wall fragments and exopolysaccharides. Selectively supports commensals (notably S. epidermidis).
On skin: the balanced, uncrowded feel of a barrier that has been fed rather than stripped.
Ceramide Complex NP + AP + EOP with phytosphingosine, cholesterol and behenic acid. Reconstitutes the stratum-corneum lamellar bilayer; EOP is the linker ceramide covalently binding to the cornified envelope.
On skin: the barrier reads whole again — softer to the touch, less porous to the next step in the routine.

Why this emulsifier for this product

The K-beauty milky-toner brief asks for near-instant absorption, minimal residue and a finish that reads as skin, not product — and the sensory panel handed the modified starch exactly that profile: the fastest absorption rate of the three (6.5), the lowest residual amount (3.6), and a powdery after-feel none of the other systems produced. The starch also reaches meaningful viscosity at low oil fractions, which is what allowed the whole formulation to sit on a lean ~8% oil phase rather than the 20–30% a conventional emulsion would need. Reducing the emulsifier from 5% to 2.5% is what makes the format lotion-light; the slight centrifuge phase-separation at that concentration is consistent with the known behaviour of the ingredient at reduced dose, and acceptable for a low-viscosity leave-on. Xanthan Gum Crosspolymer-2 provides the viscoelastic matrix that carries the ceramide and postbiotic actives without weighing the toner down.

07 · Market Positioning

Skinification meets K-beauty

Skinification

Skinification is the migration of active-driven skincare logic into adjacent categories — make-up, body care, haircare. The BB Cream and the Anti-Age Body Cream sit squarely in that current: tinted make-up carrying a multi-mechanism anti-pollution complex, and daily body care with a matrikine peptide, NAD⁺ precursor and dermal-repair actives that once belonged only to serums.

The reader now expects the same efficacy grammar from a body cream as from a facial serum, and the same protective claims from a BB cream as from a moisturiser with SPF.

K-Beauty

The Milky Toner answers the K-beauty ritual of multi-step, thin-layer hydration — a hybrid toner-emulsion applied to the transiently permeable post-cleansing barrier. Ceramide bilayer reconstitution, postbiotic microbiome support and Centella soothing are combined in a single lightweight, fast-absorbing vehicle finished with a slow-pour orifice-reducer cap for a ritualistic gesture at the point of use.

The format itself — milky, low-viscosity, layered on damp skin — is a direct import of the Korean toner category into a Western clean-beauty formulation frame.

08 · Conclusions

What the seven-series screening proved

01

Each emulsifier has a technical identity of its own.

Interfacial-film mechanics, lamellar liquid-crystal co-assembly, Pickering-type particle adsorption — three fundamentally distinct formulation spaces, expressed consistently across every one of the seven experimental series and across both the instrumental panel and the sensory jury.

02

The pairing of emulsifier to product was earned, not assumed.

Pigment compatibility, oil-phase tolerance and sensory profile determined the assignment together. No system was forced into a category the screening said it could not serve — the ester blend to the tinted facial fluid, the alkyl polyglucoside to the rich body cream, the modified starch to the lightweight toner.

03

Three finished products, stable and ready to defend.

Every prototype held pH and passed centrifugation at both t0 and t24h — a BB Cream, a Body Cream and a Milky Toner, each with a defined dispensing system, a packaging concept, and a coherent actives system built on top of a screened emulsifier.

That three natural systems sharing the same category and the same sustainability positioning generate such profoundly different formulation spaces is precisely the case for a rigorous, empirically grounded screening phase in natural emulsifier selection.

Future perspectives

Extend the three-month ambient stability study to six-to-twelve-month and accelerated (40 °C) conditions; explore secondary thickener systems to counter the viscosity loss observed on the starch-based platform; run consumer and clinical testing on the three finished prototypes; and extend the seven-series screening methodology to other natural emulsifier classes as the portfolio of bio-derived ingredients continues to grow.
